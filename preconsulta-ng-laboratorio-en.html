CORE CAPABILITIES

Our own technology for the consultation

A speech engine trained for Mexican consultations, plus verification layers that check everything before it reaches the record.

MEGI Medical v2.1

Our own speech recognition engine, trained on real consultations in Mexican Spanish. It transcribes live while the physician sees the patient.

Clinical normalizer

Turns spoken form into written form: «quinientos miligramos cada doce horas» → «500 mg every 12 h». The original text is kept.

Detect and audit, never invent

Every doubtful term, drug or value is flagged for review, with its evidence. MEGI never fills gaps on its own.

Dictated identifiers

CURP, NSS, RFC and INE dictated aloud, with structure and check-digit validation. If a letter is ambiguous, it asks.

Local SOAP scribe

Drafts the SOAP note, suggests ICD-10 codes and prescriptions, and prints documents with each physician's license number, without leaving the clinic.

Evidence with citations

Searches Clinical Practice Guidelines and over 139k Spanish FDA label texts, with a contraindication table covering 2,248 drugs.

Patient studies

Reads X-rays, ECGs, labs, breast and thyroid ultrasound and brain MRI, and delivers a pre-diagnosis for physician review.

Pre-consultation and «Still to ask»

Pre-consultation is built from age, history and the reason for the visit. During the visit, MEGI reminds live what hasn't been asked yet.

Data sovereignty and security

No cloud. Isolation per hospital and per physician, passkeys for privileged accounts, tamper-proof audit log and no clinical deletes.

How does dictation become clinical text?

Physicians speak in words; the record is written in figures and units. The normalizer converts it instantly and consistently: same input, same output.

What it normalizes:

  1. 1 Numbers and ages: «treinta y cuatro años» → 34 years
  2. 2 Doses and units: «quinientos miligramos» → 500 mg
  3. 3 Frequencies: «cada doce horas» → q12h
  4. 4 The original spoken text is kept
«What you said, the way the record writes it.»

What's still missing from this consultation?

The chief complaint triggers the questions and red flags from Clinical Practice Guidelines. MEGI matches them live against what has been said and keeps what's pending at the top.

How it decides:

  1. 1 The complaint (typed or dictated) selects the template
  2. 2 A question counts as asked once it's heard in the visit
  3. 3 A red flag needs two distinct cues: mentioning «arm» isn't enough
  4. 4 Pending alarms always come first
«It would rather remind you twice than let an alarm slip.»

What does MEGI see on a chest X-ray?

Megi Tórax checks 20 findings with calibrated thresholds and marks on the film where it found them. When it isn't sure, it says so: it separates present from possible. If the film isn't a chest X-ray or isn't readable, it abstains.

What it does:

  1. 1 Megi_tipo_estudio recognizes the study and projection
  2. 2 20 findings: cardiomegaly, effusion, consolidation, nodule, pneumothorax…
  3. 3 Localization map over the film
  4. 4 Also: Megi Columna (spine) and Megi Abdomen
«A second read in seconds, always for physician review.»

How does MEGI read an electrocardiogram?

Megi ECG works in three layers: it measures every interval, classifies 38 findings with a network trained on hundreds of thousands of ECGs, and cross-checks both: if the network says «AV block», the measured PR has to confirm it. It already runs in its reading desk and is being integrated into the consultation.

What it delivers:

  1. 1 Reads a photo or PDF of the paper, or the device file
  2. 2 HR, PR, QRS, QT/QTc and axis on the median beat
  3. 3 «Present» and «possible» findings, with named criteria (Sgarbossa…)
  4. 4 Warns when a photo lowers confidence in ST and QT
«Every diagnosis comes with the measurement behind it.»

What happens when the patient brings lab results?

The PDF, photo or file is read automatically: MEGI recognizes every analyte, compares it with the lab's own reference range and looks up what the IMSS Clinical Practice Guidelines say about those values.

What it does:

  1. 1 Reads text PDFs, photos of results or CSV
  2. 2 Uses the lab's printed range when available
  3. 3 Finds related guidelines and answers citing them
  4. 4 Everything feeds the visit's pre-diagnosis
«Out-of-range values, with the guideline right beside them.»

Is this prescription safe for this patient?

Before printing, the prescription is checked against a contraindication table covering 2,248 drugs built from FDA labels, with severity and the citation behind every alert.

What it checks:

  1. 1 Drug + patient condition (pregnancy, renal failure…)
  2. 2 Interactions between the prescribed drugs
  3. 3 High-confidence alerts only
  4. 4 Every alert carries its source so it can be verified
«Alerts come with their evidence, not as a black box.»
